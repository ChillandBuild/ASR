# Roman Tanglish annotation policy — corpus preparation revision 1

Purpose: audio-faithful Tamil–English transcription in Roman letters. This policy governs new human adjudications; it does not rewrite the existing pilot references or their scores.

1. Transcribe what is actually audible. Preserve colloquial Tamil, spoken English, repetitions and meaningful hesitation. Do not translate, summarize, formalize Tamil grammar, or add words to make a sentence read better.
2. Keep the extracted `reference` unchanged. Put a complete corrected transcript in `adjudicated_reference`, not merely the changed words. If no correction is needed, leave adjudication empty.
3. Use Roman letters for Tamil. Within a reviewed batch, use the same spelling for the same audible Tamil word. Preserve vowel length where it distinguishes words. Do not automatically treat `amma` and `aama`, negated and affirmative phrases, or different grammatical endings as equivalent. When spelling is uncertain, explain it in notes rather than guessing.
4. Keep ordinary English words in their conventional English spelling when identifiable from audio. Preserve audible Tamil suffixes consistently; for new corrections prefer explicit forms such as `office-ku` and `meeting-oda`. This is an annotation convention, not an automatic conversion applied to old references.
5. Preserve the actual value of numbers, dates and amounts. For new corrections use Arabic digits for clearly identified amounts or counts, and record uncertainty. Do not modify an old approved numeric reference just to match a model. Future digit normalization must be a separately logged scoring pipeline.
6. Record names as confidently identified from the audio. If a name's spelling is uncertain, keep the clip pending or explain the ambiguity. Do not infer identities from document titles or from the fact that a name was spoken.
7. Listen to the entire clip. Check that its start/end boundaries contain all words in the reference and no unlabelled continuation. If a word is cut, speakers overlap, music obscures speech, or transcription is uncertain, keep it pending and note the problem. Boundary repair is a separate alignment task; do not change offsets in the review CSV.
8. Assign anonymous voice IDs only after listening. Use one consistent ID for a recurring voice across every recording. Multiple audible speakers require all their IDs, e.g. `["voice_01", "voice_02"]`. Unknown voices remain `[]`; unknown speakers cannot support a speaker-independent split.
9. Set `review_status=approved` only after the audio and final reference have been checked. Enter your real reviewer identifier and a timezone-aware actual review timestamp. Do not reuse the old 122-clip attestation for new clips.

Training subset selection follows actual review and speaker annotation. All clips sharing any speaker must stay in the same partition; clips connecting multiple speakers can connect whole groups. The original 122 clips are an exposed development benchmark. The current corpus is not certified as a pristine final test. An exploratory train/validation experiment is possible once a sufficient reviewed, speaker-separated subset exists; a new final test remains a separate requirement for stronger claims.
